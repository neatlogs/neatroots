# Proposed community code of conduct

Status: proposal for maintainer review; not an adopted project policy.

Adoption requires verified private reporting contacts: a primary contact and an
alternate for conflicts of interest. Maintainers must establish those contacts,
update the reporting instructions, and approve promotion of this document to the
root `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` before it becomes the project code of conduct.

neatroots welcomes people with different backgrounds, identities, abilities, and
levels of experience. Everyone taking part should be able to ask questions,
contribute, and disagree without being targeted or intimidated.

## Community standards

- Be respectful and patient. Explain unfamiliar terms and make room for newcomers.
- Critique ideas, code, and evidence rather than attacking people. Explain your
  reasoning, acknowledge uncertainty, and accept corrections.
- Keep discussion relevant and constructive. Respect others' time and boundaries,
  including requests to stop unwanted contact.
- Use inclusive language and respect how people identify themselves.

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sexual harassment, and degrading personal
attacks are unacceptable. So are publishing or threatening to publish someone's
private information (doxxing), repeated unwanted contact, and retaliation against
someone for raising a concern or participating in its review.

## Scope

These standards apply in project-managed spaces, including this repository's
issues, pull requests, and discussions. They also apply when someone officially
represents neatroots in another space, such as an event or a project account.

## Raising a concern

No dedicated private project reporting channel is listed yet. Before adopting
this proposal, maintainers need to designate both a private project contact and
an alternate contact for concerns involving that contact or another conflict of
interest. The options below do not provide a complete project reporting process,
particularly for conduct outside GitHub or concerns involving repository admins.

Do not open a public issue or discussion to report an incident, name the people
involved publicly, or post private information as evidence.

For content on GitHub, use **Report content → Report to repository admins** only
when GitHub offers that option. This sends a report to repository admins; it is
not an independent route for a concern involving those admins.

Separately, you can report violations of GitHub's rules to GitHub Support through
its [abuse reporting form](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse).
See GitHub's [reporting abuse or spam guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
for available reporting options. GitHub Support handles platform abuse under
GitHub's rules; contacting it does not initiate a neatroots enforcement review.

## Project response

Project stewardship follows [MAINTAINERS.md](../../MAINTAINERS.md). Under this proposal,
maintainers and the core team are responsible for addressing conduct in spaces
they manage, using the moderation tools available to them.

Responses should consider the severity, impact, and repetition of the conduct.
They may include a request to change behavior, removal of content, a warning,
or temporary or permanent restrictions on participation. A serious incident may
warrant immediate restrictions. Ordinary good-faith disagreement is welcome.

People reviewing a concern should avoid handling cases in which they have a
conflict of interest and limit sharing of personal details to what is needed to
assess and address it. The private contact and alternate review route still need
to be established before this proposal can serve as an adopted reporting policy.
